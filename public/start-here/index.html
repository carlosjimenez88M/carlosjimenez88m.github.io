My research and writing follow two primary lines: AI Software Engineering and narrative arcs in music. One examines how to build and operate AI applications; the other asks how songs and albums develop stories and arguments, and how computational methods can help us understand them.

Narrative arcs in music#

Start with Attention Windows: Beatles vs Pink Floyd, which examines semantic persistence in lyrics and the gap between lexical similarity and thematic coherence.

My broader questions concern how ideas unfold across a song or an album: what persists, what changes, and how repetition or shifts in perspective shape an arc. NLP, embeddings, and LLMs are tools for investigating those questions, with interpretation and statistical scrutiny guiding what their results mean.

The Narrative Arcs in Music index collects this research and its published essays.

AI Software Engineering#

This line focuses on prompts, evaluations, agents, MLOps, cloud infrastructure, and inference on constrained hardware. The AI Software Engineering index collects the writing. The following paths offer places to begin.

Prompts, evaluation, and LLMOps#

Start with MLflow for AI Engineering: Prompts Are Release Artifacts. It follows a prompt change from registration and tracing through evaluation, optimization, and release, using MLflow 3.16.0.

The recurring question is simple: what evidence should we require before changing the behavior of an AI application?

Inference, architecture, and the edge#

AI Architecture: Training and Inference explores hardware choices. Edge Computing and Edge Machine Learning and MLOps on Raspberry Pi 5 bring those questions to smaller devices.

The Edge + Agentic AI section collects this line of work.
