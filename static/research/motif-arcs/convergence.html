One motif, several questions

Close in space, close in meaning?

Two passages can share vocabulary, characters and a situation while making incompatible claims. They can also use different words to maintain the same position. Reading these curves requires returning to the proposition: what is claimed, who claims it, and when it applies.

1. Semantic proximity

Cosine similarity compares the vectors of two complete text blocks. Here it helps locate echoes; it does not decide whether their claims are compatible.

2. Relations between ideas

To identify opposition, we need to fix the referents, scope, time and modality. A different participant or date does not, by itself, create a contradiction.

3. Agreement between readers

When two providers assign the same label, that shows consistency between their readings. It neither replaces a justification nor makes the label a literary truth.

The event field has a design problem

The original schema required a choice among actual, hypothetical, quoted, recalled and none. It mixes two questions: whether something happened and how it is presented. An event that happened can also be quoted or remembered. A difference in this field may therefore reflect a different interpretation, overlapping categories, or both.

With all retained blocks, the readers agree on 8/8 relation labels, 7/8 voice labels and 2/8 event labels. That contrast calls for revisiting the questions as well as the answers.

These are eight cases selected after the pilot, with two different context budgets. The displayed labels pass the source-address and target-pair binding checks; those checks do not verify that the text supports the interpretation. These are neither accuracy measurements nor independent tests of generalization.

A smaller, more explicit control

Separate the topic from what the sentence claims

All the texts can be read here: 56 original sentences in four families, each with Spanish and English versions. Every template compares a base sentence with six variations. The negation is intended to be incompatible with the base when referents, time and scope remain fixed; changes in participant, time and modality require different questions.

What this control supports

In three of the eight templates, small ranks the negation above the paraphrase; large does not do so in these templates. This reveals a specific sensitivity to phrasing. It does not establish an overall winner: the eight templates share four families, and negation changes fewer words than paraphrasing.

Lexical overlap has not been controlled for, and no threshold has been fitted. A high score for a temporal or modal variation is not necessarily an error: it preserves the topic while changing the time or strength of the claim. The control helps formulate a question that distance alone cannot answer.

Data behind this view

This view uses previously computed vectors and saved readings. Changing a selection makes no provider calls. The data file contains cosine similarities, block identifiers, labels, authored interpretive paraphrases and original control sentences. It contains no song lyrics, credentials or extended provider responses.
